# Creative Intelligence

Use this reference for Facebook/Instagram ad creative, copy, image, video, and Reels analysis.

## Creative analysis framework

Break each ad into:

1. Hook
2. Problem/pain point
3. Solution/product
4. Core benefit
5. Proof/social proof
6. Offer
7. Objection handling
8. CTA

Mark each item as observed, inferred, or unknown when evidence is incomplete.

## Hook analysis

Classify hooks when evidence supports it:

- Problem/pain
- Benefit/result
- Curiosity
- Demonstration
- Contrarian
- Social proof
- Offer/price
- Story
- Question
- Before/after

Do not call a hook "winning" unless performance data supports that conclusion.

## Image analysis

Inspect, where possible:

- product visibility
- subject placement
- visual hierarchy
- contrast/readability
- text overlay
- brand consistency
- offer prominence
- proof elements
- CTA visibility
- mobile-first legibility

## Video/Reels analysis

When a video is available, inspect:

- first 1–3 seconds
- opening visual
- spoken/text hook
- pacing
- scene changes
- product demonstration
- captions
- voiceover
- proof
- offer
- final CTA

Produce a timestamped breakdown when timestamps are available.

## Creative completeness score

A completeness score may be useful for comparing how many message components are observable. It is not a prediction of ROAS, CPA, or profitability.

## Performance-aware ranking

When performance data exists, consider:

- spend
- conversions
- CPA
- ROAS
- CTR
- conversion rate
- sample size
- date range

Flag small spend or conversion counts. Do not let a tiny sample automatically become the winner.

## Creative fatigue

Possible fatigue signals include:

- CTR declining across comparable periods
- frequency increasing
- CPA increasing
- conversion rate weakening

Treat these as a diagnostic signal. Validate against spend, audience size, placement, delivery changes, seasonality, and tracking quality.

## Creative generation

When asked for new creatives:

1. Extract the strongest observed/inferred market patterns.
2. Identify overused angles.
3. Identify plausible differentiation opportunities.
4. Generate original hooks and concepts.
5. Produce copy, storyboard, or creative brief as requested.

Do not reproduce another advertiser's creative verbatim, branding, testimonial, or identity.
