# Advanced Competitor Intelligence

Use this module for public Meta Ad Library and browser-observed competitor research.

## Research workflow

1. Identify the competitor/brand.
2. Open the public Ad Library or other public source through the available browser capability.
3. Capture only observable information.
4. Record source URL and observation date when possible.
5. Group ads by format, hook, angle, offer, proof, and CTA.
6. Compare competitors.
7. Identify recurring market patterns.
8. Generate market-gap hypotheses.
9. Convert hypotheses into original creative tests.

## Capture fields

- brand
- ad/library ID when visible
- source URL
- active/inactive status when visible
- first/last seen dates when visible
- format
- hook/hook type
- primary text
- headline
- offer
- angle
- proof
- CTA
- landing page URL when publicly accessible
- notes

## Ad longevity

A long-running or repeatedly observed ad may be a useful signal of continued use, but longevity does not prove profitability.

## Competitor comparison

Compare:

- observed ad count
- active observed count
- format mix
- recurring hooks
- recurring angles
- offers
- CTAs
- proof patterns
- landing-page/message alignment

Never convert observed ad count into estimated spend or market share.

## Market saturation

A saturation statement must be scoped to the captured sample.

Good:

> 14 of 20 captured ads used discount-led offers.

Bad:

> 70% of the market uses discounts.

## Market gaps

A gap is a test hypothesis when an angle or format was not observed in the supplied sample.

It is not proof that competitors never use it.

## Originality

Use competitor research to understand patterns and differentiation opportunities. Generate original creative concepts rather than copying competitor copy, branding, testimonials, or distinctive creative execution.

## Browser-first behavior

When browser access is available, prefer the user's existing authenticated/public browsing session over requiring an API token for public competitor research. Do not request passwords, session cookies, or 2FA codes in chat.
